First, your English is already quite understandable. There are a few places where I would adjust it:

“reports on testpaper, newspaper or somewhere else” → reports in exam papers, newspapers, or elsewhere
“whose topics are regularly about...” → whose topics often cover...
“the news happened in fields above impacting...” → how events in these fields affect society, the economy, and people's lives
“engage the other side of this” → explore the other side of this / get involved in the discussions behind these reports
“source of reports” → sources for these reports
“whether it a good step” → whether it is a good first step
“completely new to all of the things” → completely new to these fields

A more natural version would be:
I've read a lot of reports in exam papers, newspapers, and elsewhere. Their topics often cover agriculture, science and technology, environmental protection, international politics, and how events in these fields affect society, the economy, and people's lives.

I know that publications such as The Economist and BBC News are common sources for these reports, but I want to explore the other side of this: are there online communities where people actually discuss these topics in depth?

I've subscribed to a few YouTube channels and joined some Reddit communities, such as economics and embedded engineering. But I'm not sure whether this is a good way to start. After all, I'm completely new to many of these fields.

Frisk, I'm thinking about building my own information network these days. Do you have any advice?
